After fire, your home is exposed to weather, theft, and pest entry until emergency board-up and tarping protect the structure.

When fire strikes a home in Garland, the first hours are chaos and crisis. Firefighting efforts create broken windows, damaged doors, shattered skylights, and missing sections of roof. The interior is exposed to wind, rain, animals, and potential theft before you even leave the scene. Before any cleanup or restoration planning can happen, the structure must be secured.

Emergency board-up and tarping is not cosmetic work or delayed repair. It is a critical emergency service that must happen within hours of the fire to prevent massive secondary losses. Rain can soak insulation and drywall throughout the home. Cold wind can cause additional damage to weakened components. Squirrels, birds, and rodents can enter through gaps and establish nests inside the walls. Opportunistic thieves can access an open, darkened home and steal belongings and copper wiring before you have a chance to assess the damage.

The sooner a professional secures the structure with plywood and heavy-duty tarps, the better your insurance claim holds up and the smaller your total repair bill becomes. Securing the property is the first professional step in your recovery.

The Critical First 24 Hours After Fire

Most insurance companies expect to see a Garland fire damage home boarded and tarped within the first twenty-four hours of the fire.

This expectation exists because water damage from rain can quickly exceed fire damage in scope and cost. A single heavy rain entering an unsecured attic can soak insulation, saturate drywall, and penetrate walls deeply enough to cause mold growth within forty-eight hours. Insurance adjusters understand this risk and look for evidence that the property was secured immediately. Delays in boarding send a signal to the adjuster that the property is not being actively managed, which can complicate claim approval.

A professional crew arrives at your Garland home within hours of the fire call with plywood sheets in various sizes, heavy-duty tarps, tarring materials, fasteners, safety equipment, and the specialized know-how to work safely around active fire debris, hazardous materials, and unstable structures. They do not remove items from inside the home or enter restricted areas damaged by fire. Their focused mission is a quick, temporary seal of all exterior openings and roof damage. This focused approach gets the critical protection in place fast.

Broken Windows and Damaged Doors

Windows are the most common entry point for water intrusion and unwanted entry into a fire-damaged home.

Fire often breaks glass outright. Even when glass remains intact, heat can warp window frames so they no longer seal properly. Frames may be split or cracked, and sashes may jam or refuse to close. Professional crews in Garland measure each window opening and cut three-quarter-inch plywood to fit snugly inside the frame or over the exterior opening. Sheets are secured with construction bolts or corrosion-resistant nails driven through metal reinforcement plates to prevent prying.

Exterior doors must be secured with equal care. If the door frame is damaged, plywood or steel covers go over the entire opening. Sliding glass doors, French doors, and garage doors all receive the same attention. A solid, visible seal of all entry points sends a message that the property is under professional management. It also makes the home less attractive to thieves and vandals who seek properties that appear abandoned and unprotected.

Protecting the Roof and Preventing Water Intrusion

A hole in the roof is among the most damaging conditions an insurance adjuster can find in a fire-damaged home.

Water enters the attic through roof openings. It soaks fiberglass insulation, which loses all effectiveness once saturated. Water flows down the inside of exterior walls and ceiling cavities, soaking drywall and insulation throughout the structure. Within two to three days of continuous moisture exposure, mold begins growing in these dark, warm cavities. Mold remediation can cost tens of thousands of dollars and can affect the entire home.

Our crew uses heavy-duty tarps secured with rope or metal straps across the damaged roofline. The tarp extends several feet past the damage on all sides to catch water that runs off the roof slope during rain. Corners and edges are tied down with extra care and redundancy to prevent wind from lifting or shifting the cover during storms. The tarping is intentionally visible and maintained throughout the restoration process. It signals to neighbors, city officials, and potential opportunists that work is underway and the property is being actively managed by professionals.

Securing Roof Penetrations and Vents

Roof damage from fire often extends to HVAC vents, exhaust pipes, chimney flues, and utility conduits that penetrate the roofline.

Each of these openings is a direct path for water to enter the attic space. Rain running down the roof slope will flow directly into the home through an unsecured vent or pipe. The tarping process includes careful sealing around all these penetrations using flashing, temporary closures, or wrap-around techniques. The goal is to create a continuous waterproof barrier across the entire roof damage area, with no gaps where water can find a way in.

Professional boarding also addresses ground-level penetrations that are often overlooked. Gas meter boxes, electrical service panels, HVAC condensate lines, and exterior drain pipes are wrapped or covered to prevent standing water, rain splash, or contaminated runoff from entering the home. These details are easy to miss during emergency response but can prevent water intrusion and secondary damage if done correctly during the initial boarding.

Preventing Animal and Pest Entry

Open windows and damaged doors invite raccoons, possums, rats, squirrels, insects, and birds into the fire-damaged home.

A Garland property left unboarded overnight can host a colony of carpenter ants, termites, or squirrels inside the walls and attic by morning. Rodents nest in fire-damaged insulation and chew through electrical wiring, plumbing pipes, and structural wood. This animal activity creates additional damage and increases future fire risk when rodents chew through live electrical wires. It also creates a new pest control problem that must be addressed separately.

Proper boarding and tarping, with careful sealing of gaps around boarded windows, elimination of openings in roof tarps, and closure of foundation vents and other gaps, eliminates entry routes for animals. This is both a damage prevention measure and a health and safety measure. It saves money on pest removal and animal damage remediation later, while also protecting your family and your crew from encounters with displaced animals when restoration work begins.

Protecting Remaining Personal Property

Even if your household belongings are not yet removed from the home, boarding the windows and doors protects them from additional rain damage and contamination.

Smoke-damaged items are often salvageable if kept dry during the restoration process. Contents restoration and cleaning is time-consuming and requires the right environment. A properly boarded-up home stays drier and stays protected from further weather damage while your restoration team plans the contents pack-out and cleaning process. Items that might otherwise be total losses due to rain damage can be recovered and cleaned if the structure stays dry.

This protection also provides a practical deterrent against theft. A darkened, sealed home with visible plywood boarding and tarps sends a clear signal that the property is actively managed and valuables are not an easy target. Properties that appear abandoned and unsecured are far more attractive to thieves and scavengers looking for quick gains. Professional boarding discourages this type of opportunistic theft.

Communication with Insurance Adjusters

Your insurance company may require board-up and tarping work to be completed before approving reconstruction payments and authorization for further work.

Many homeowner insurance policies specifically include emergency boarding and tarping services as covered emergency response items. Professional crews coordinate directly with your insurance adjuster, providing photos and detailed documentation showing exactly what was secured. This paperwork becomes part of your official claim record and helps justify the scope of future restoration work to the insurance company.

If your insurance policy covers emergency services like board-up, your deductible may not apply to these initial boarding costs. Some policies charge a flat emergency service fee instead of applying the full deductible. Always check your policy documents or contact your adjuster before the boarding work begins to understand the financial terms and whether emergency board-up is covered.

Temporary Protection While Planning Restoration

Once boarding and tarping are in place, comprehensive restoration planning can move forward without weather risk.

Structural assessments, water damage evaluation, contents inventory, and restoration strategy can all happen while the property is properly protected. You and your restoration team have time to make sound decisions about what can be repaired versus what must be replaced, how to negotiate with insurance, and what sequence of work makes the most sense for your situation.

Boarding and tarping is intentionally temporary. It is not the final restoration. It buys critical time for you and the restoration professionals to evaluate damage thoroughly, obtain proper permits if needed, coordinate with multiple contractors, and plan every phase of the rebuild. Many Garland homeowners find that proper emergency boarding in the first hours prevents complications and additional damage that would cost thousands of dollars more to address if the structure had been left exposed.
