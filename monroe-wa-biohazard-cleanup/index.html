Why Household Cleaning Cannot Eliminate the Danger

Cleaning and disinfection are fundamentally different processes that serve different purposes in restoring safety. Cleaning removes dirt, dust, grease, and visible soiling from surfaces. A clean surface looks spotless and inviting to family members and visitors. Yet visual cleanliness provides no protection against infectious pathogens invisible to the human eye. Disinfection actively kills microorganisms using chemical agents proven to break down pathogen cell structures and eliminate viability. Household cleaners may contain some disinfectant ingredients, but in concentrations far too weak to be effective against serious pathogens. The product must contact every contaminated surface and remain wet for a specific duration while the chemical does its work at the molecular level. Household products and cleaning techniques do not meet these requirements. Monroe families who attempt to clean infectious disease contamination put themselves and others at ongoing risk of illness transmission.
