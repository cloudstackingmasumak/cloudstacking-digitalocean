HEPA stands for High-Efficiency Particulate Air. HEPA filters use a dense mesh of randomly arranged fibers that capture particles through three mechanisms: interception (particles following air streams that pass within one fiber diameter of the surface), impaction (larger particles unable to follow the air stream and crashing into the filter), and diffusion (smallest particles bouncing randomly and eventually striking fibers).

The standard for HEPA filtration requires capturing 99. 97 percent of particles 0. 3 microns or larger. This size threshold is important because it targets the most dangerous particles. Bacteria typically range from 0. 5 to 5 microns, so HEPA filters catch almost all bacterial particles. Virus particles are smaller, but they usually travel attached to respiratory droplets or organic material that is larger than 0. 3 microns, making them effectively captured.
