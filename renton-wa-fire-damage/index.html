Fire hose water isn't clean city water. It picks up ash, soot, chemicals, and contamination as it flows through burned materials and over charred debris. This contaminated water soaks into everything porous: wood, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete.

Water spreads horizontally and vertically through capillary action. You might see standing water only in the kitchen, but that water has wicked up into wall cavities two stories high. Monitoring Moisture Content During Drying

Professional restoration companies use moisture meters to measure the water content of affected materials. Readings are taken daily and tracked on charts. These charts show whether drying is progressing at an appropriate rate or if additional measures are needed. If materials aren't drying fast enough, additional dehumidifiers are added or air movers are repositioned.

Different materials have different acceptable moisture levels once dry. Drywall should reach 12 to 15 percent before finishing. Wood should be 12 to 15 percent. Concrete and masonry can accept slightly higher levels, up to 18 to 20 percent. Professional crews tracks these specific target levels, not just general "dryness."
